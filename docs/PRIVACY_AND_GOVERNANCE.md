# Privacy and governance

This pilot accepts organizational public records and permissioned **aggregate** program data. It is not a beneficiary case-management system. Beneficiary identifiers, unsupported sensitive columns, formulas, oversized files and suspicious document instructions are rejected/quarantined. These checks reduce risk; a pattern scan is not proof of de-identification. Contributors remain responsible for reviewing aggregates and obtaining lawful permission before sharing.

Default visibility is private. Owners grant distinct purposes: benchmarking, pooled analysis, donor access, publication and external AI. Recipient, cause, geography, expiry and attribution apply together; unsupported combinations fail closed. All restricted inputs must permit a derived use. Broad administrative roles do not bypass private source policy. Source access, export and background execution recheck current authorization.

Pooled releases use fixed outcome/period/cause/geography cohort sets, documented independence, consistent definitions/populations/interventions/follow-up, at least 3 independent organizations and at least 10 participants in every disclosed binary cell **and complement**. Suppression returns no contributing totals/rates/citations that reconstruct a hidden cell. Arbitrary subsets/filters and overlapping/unknown cohorts are rejected. These are configurable product thresholds, not legal guarantees of anonymity. No causal claim or unsupported significance is calculated.

Reviewers are explicitly assigned to a revision. Trusted reviewer provisioning requires a platform operator. A writer in the contributing workspace cannot independently approve its output. Third-party evidence retains its original recipient restrictions even when another owner assigns a reviewer. Review records retain reviewer, reason, timestamp and source/grant snapshots. Demo approvals are demonstrative; a qualified real human review process must be staffed during the pilot.

Withdrawal immediately disables source use and derivative serving. Source locks serialize competing finalizations/edits; queued jobs reauthorize, stale leases cannot commit, and affected alerts/approvals/graph paths are unavailable. A separate job physically removes raw files and sensitive normalized content, with observable status and bounded retries. Raw uploads also expire after 24 hours. Source/audit tombstones retain minimal operational IDs; audit records are application-enforced append-only, not cryptographically tamper-proof.

A source revision or revoked grant invalidates dependent artifacts. Identity proof changes, withdrawal or withdrawn review remove the workspace linkage until new verification. Prior exports cannot be remotely recalled. Backups may retain deleted material until the operator's defined retention window expires; restore procedures must reapply the withdrawal ledger before serving restored data. Production operators must document retention, deletion deadlines, backup encryption/access and incident response; the local demo does not promise encrypted storage.

No third-party analytics or external AI processing is enabled by default. The optional provider receives only already authorized source-backed claim text after separate external-processing grants. It selects grounded claims; it has no autonomous tools. Logs carry correlation IDs/statuses and safe diagnostics, not raw uploads or full private prompts. Outbound email, introductions, applications and payments are not silently sent.

Institutional subscriptions and sponsored data preparation may support operations; core contributor functions stay free. Contributions never buy ranking position. Real adoption, grants funded, implementation savings and outcome changes require opt-in human measurement, with uncertainty and attribution limitations retained. A governance council, ethics review program, external security certification and international legal coverage are future operational work, not accomplishments of this build.
